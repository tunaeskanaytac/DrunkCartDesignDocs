# Progression Design

## Purpose

The progression system should make each run feel like a transformation.

The team should begin as:

> **A relatively normal person driving a shopping cart and another person shooting from it.**

By the end of the run, they should become:

> **A highly specialized, extremely powerful, and increasingly uncontrollable machine of destruction.**

The progression system should reinforce the game's central relationship:

```text id="z3qj4e"
Power
    ↓
Risk
    ↓
Chaos
    ↓
Adaptation
    ↓
More Power
```

The key design principle is:

> **Progression should change what the players do, not merely increase their numbers.**

---

# Progression Structure

The game should have multiple layers of progression.

```text id="6id8tq"
Moment-to-Moment Rewards
        ↓
Run Progression
        ↓
Build Development
        ↓
Run Completion
        ↓
Meta-Progression
```

Each layer should serve a different purpose.

---

# Layer 1 — Moment-to-Moment Rewards

During combat, players receive immediate rewards.

Possible rewards:

* Experience
* Beer
* Temporary powerups
* Health
* Currency
* Upgrade materials

The basic loop is:

```text id="5a1m7f"
Fight
    ↓
Kill
    ↓
Collect
    ↓
Make a Decision
    ↓
Become Stronger
```

The important part is that rewards should create decisions.

---

# Layer 2 — Run Progression

During a run, the team grows stronger.

The basic progression loop:

```text id="m6j7jv"
Kill Enemies
    ↓
Gain Experience
    ↓
Level Up
    ↓
Choose Upgrade
    ↓
Build Becomes Stronger
    ↓
Enemies Become More Dangerous
```

The team should be significantly different at the end of a run compared to the beginning.

---

# Shared Leveling System

The simplest initial system is:

> **The entire team shares one experience bar.**

When the team levels up:

```text id="kmbqoa"
Team Levels Up
        ↓
Upgrade Choices Appear
        ↓
Players Choose an Upgrade
        ↓
Build Changes
```

This prevents the Rider and Shooter from becoming permanently separated progression systems.

---

# Upgrade Choice Structure

At each level-up, the team receives a selection of upgrades.

Example:

```text id="b7k9x4"
LEVEL UP

[ Shooter Upgrade ]

[ Rider Upgrade ]

[ Shared Upgrade ]
```

This creates a direct choice between:

* Increasing offensive power
* Improving vehicle control
* Strengthening the entire team

The choice should ideally require discussion.

Example:

> **Shooter:** "We need more damage."

> **Rider:** "We need better brakes."

> **Shooter:** "I can kill them before we need brakes."

> **Rider:** "You said that last time."

---

# Alternative: Shared Choices with Player Ownership

Another option is to present several upgrade choices, but each upgrade belongs to one player.

Example:

```text id="6eyqck"
[ Double Shot ]
Shooter Upgrade

[ Better Braking ]
Rider Upgrade

[ Intoxication Resistance ]
Shared Upgrade
```

The players then decide together.

This is probably the best initial system because it makes progression itself cooperative.

---

# Upgrade Categories

There should be four major categories.

```text id="q9r8m2"
Shooter Upgrades
        +
Rider Upgrades
        +
Shared Upgrades
        +
Beer / Intoxication Upgrades
```

Each category should serve a different purpose.

---

# Shooter Upgrades

Shooter upgrades improve combat.

Categories include:

## Damage

* Increased base damage
* Increased critical damage
* Increased damage against marked targets

---

## Attack Speed

* Faster fire rate
* Reduced cooldown
* Faster reload

---

## Projectile

* Additional projectiles
* Pierce
* Larger projectiles
* Homing
* Explosions

---

## Targeting

* Improved accuracy
* Critical hits
* Weak-point damage
* Target prioritization

---

# Example Shooter Upgrades

## Double Shot

> Fire an additional projectile.

---

## Piercing Rounds

> Projectiles pass through enemies.

---

## Heavy Caliber

> Increased damage but reduced fire rate.

---

## Drive-By

> Gain increased fire rate while the cart is moving quickly.

---

## Momentum Shot

> Damage increases with the cart's current speed.

---

## Covering Fire

> Killing enemies near the cart temporarily reduces incoming damage.

---

# Rider Upgrades

Rider upgrades improve movement and vehicle combat.

Categories include:

## Mobility

* Acceleration
* Maximum speed
* Boost
* Turning

---

## Control

* Braking
* Traction
* Drift
* Intoxication resistance

---

## Impact

* Collision damage
* Knockback
* Armor
* Impact radius

---

# Example Rider Upgrades

## Reinforced Frame

> Take less damage from collisions.

---

## Spiked Front

> Deal additional damage when ramming enemies.

---

## Turbo Engine

> Increase maximum speed.

---

## Emergency Brake

> Perform a powerful brake maneuver with a cooldown.

---

## Greased Wheels

> Increase speed but reduce traction.

---

## Drunk Driver

> Improve handling as intoxication increases.

---

# Shared Upgrades

Shared upgrades should affect both players or the entire team.

Examples:

## Experience Magnet

> Increase experience collection range.

---

## Teamwork

> Both players gain a bonus while near their ideal positions.

---

## Shared Momentum

> Certain Shooter attacks become stronger based on cart speed.

---

## Last Stand

> When the cart is nearly destroyed, both players gain temporary power.

---

## Efficient Drinking

> Beer provides more benefits without increasing intoxication as quickly.

---

# Beer Upgrades

Beer upgrades should modify the relationship between power and control.

Examples:

## Stronger Drinks

> Beer provides more combat power.

---

## Lightweight

> Intoxication increases more slowly.

---

## Reckless Drinking

> Beer provides significantly more power but increases intoxication faster.

---

## Functional Alcoholic

> Gain improved handling at high intoxication.

---

## Last Call

> Gain a powerful temporary effect when reaching a new intoxication threshold.

---

# Beer as a Progression System

Beer should create its own progression axis.

The team should have to decide:

```text id="qj2xq4"
More Beer
    ↓
More Power
    ↓
More Intoxication
    ↓
More Handling Difficulty
```

The question is not:

> **Should we drink beer?**

The question is:

> **How much beer can we handle?**

---

# Intoxication Thresholds

The game can use thresholds rather than a purely continuous system.

Example:

```text id="5s0gkk"
0% ───────── Sober
25% ──────── Tipsy
50% ──────── Drunk
75% ──────── Very Drunk
100% ────── Wasted
```

Each threshold can unlock:

* Combat bonuses
* New effects
* New handling challenges
* Unique upgrades

---

# Threshold Example

## Sober

No special effects.

---

## Tipsy

* +10% Shooter damage
* Slightly increased cart momentum

---

## Drunk

* +25% Shooter damage
* +10% attack speed
* Reduced traction

---

## Very Drunk

* +50% Shooter damage
* Special weapon effects
* Difficult braking

---

## Wasted

* Extreme combat power
* Major handling changes
* Access to unique abilities

---

# Beer Should Not Only Be a Penalty

The Shooter should not feel as if they are simply accepting a debuff to become stronger.

Beer should create new possibilities.

For example:

> **At high intoxication, the Shooter gains access to special attacks.**

Or:

> **The Rider gains new vehicle abilities when intoxication is high.**

This changes the relationship from:

```text
Beer
    =
Power + Penalty
```

Into:

```text
Beer
    =
New Playstyle
```

---

# Build Archetypes

Progression should create recognizable builds.

---

## Machine Gun Drunk

### Shooter

* High fire rate
* Multiple projectiles
* High intoxication

### Rider

* Intoxication handling
* High mobility

### Gameplay

> The team moves quickly while the Shooter fills the screen with projectiles.

---

## Demolition Cart

### Rider

* High speed
* Collision damage
* Armor

### Shooter

* Effects triggered by cart collisions

### Gameplay

> The cart itself becomes the main weapon.

---

## Precision Team

### Shooter

* High single-target damage
* Critical hits

### Rider

* Stable handling
* Strong braking

### Gameplay

> The Rider carefully positions the cart while the Shooter eliminates dangerous targets.

---

## Chaos Team

### Shooter

* Random effects
* Explosions
* Chain reactions

### Rider

* High mobility
* Drift

### Gameplay

> The team embraces unpredictability.

---

## Beer Synergy Team

### Shooter

* High intoxication bonuses

### Rider

* Improved high-intoxication handling

### Gameplay

> The team intentionally becomes more powerful as they become more drunk.

---

# Upgrade Synergy

The most interesting upgrades should interact across roles.

## Example: Momentum Shot

### Shooter Upgrade

Damage increases with cart speed.

### Rider Response

The Rider wants to maintain speed.

Result:

```text id="f3d8w8"
Rider Maintains Momentum
        ↓
Shooter Deals More Damage
        ↓
Enemies Die Faster
        ↓
More Rewards
```

---

## Example: Impact Mark

### Rider

Rams an enemy.

### Shooter

Deals bonus damage to the impacted enemy.

Result:

```text id="qg7x13"
Rider Ramming
        ↓
Enemy Becomes Vulnerable
        ↓
Shooter Finishes Enemy
```

---

## Example: Drunk Driver

### Shooter

Becomes stronger with intoxication.

### Rider

Handles intoxicated cart better.

Result:

```text id="v7wz3m"
More Drinking
        ↓
More Shooter Power
        ↓
More Vehicle Difficulty
        ↓
Rider Gains Handling Benefits
        ↓
Team Can Sustain High Intoxication
```

---

# Upgrade Synergy Tiers

Some upgrades could become stronger when combined.

Example:

```text id="f44z5p"
Momentum Shot
    +
Turbo Engine
    ↓
High-Speed Combat Build
```

Or:

```text id="k2u7jh"
Spiked Front
    +
Impact Mark
    ↓
Ram-and-Finish Build
```

Or:

```text id="l0l0a1"
Drunk Driver
    +
Drunken Fury
    ↓
High-Intoxication Build
```

The game should encourage players to recognize these synergies.

---

# Upgrade Rarity

Upgrades can eventually have rarity levels.

Example:

```text id="e3u5k0"
Common
    ↓
Uncommon
    ↓
Rare
    ↓
Legendary
```

However, rarity should not be added until the basic upgrade system works.

A rare upgrade should ideally be:

> **A new gameplay possibility.**

Not simply:

> **+50% damage instead of +20%.**

---

# Transformative Upgrades

Some upgrades should fundamentally change gameplay.

Examples:

## No Brakes

> The cart cannot brake, but gains massive acceleration and collision damage.

---

## Passenger Cannon

> The Shooter can launch themselves from the cart temporarily.

---

## Explosive Cart

> The cart explodes after reaching maximum momentum.

---

## Rolling Arsenal

> The cart's weapons fire automatically while drifting.

---

## Liquid Courage

> The Shooter becomes stronger whenever the Rider performs a dangerous maneuver.

These upgrades should create new team strategies.

---

# The Build Should Create New Communication

Progression should affect how the players communicate.

Examples:

### Speed Build

> "Keep going!"

### Precision Build

> "Slow down!"

### Ram Build

> "Line me up!"

### Beer Build

> "Drink!"

### Defensive Build

> "Get us out of here!"

The best upgrades should change the team's behavior.

---

# Progression Pacing

A run should provide upgrades frequently enough to maintain excitement.

A possible structure:

```text id="3j7k5w"
Start
    ↓
First Upgrade
    ↓
First Beer
    ↓
Second Upgrade
    ↓
Intoxication Threshold
    ↓
Third Upgrade
    ↓
Elite Encounter
    ↓
Major Upgrade
    ↓
High Intoxication
    ↓
Final Challenge
```

The exact timing should be tuned through playtesting.

---

# Early-Run Progression

The beginning of the run should establish the build.

The team should quickly receive:

* A first upgrade
* A first beer decision
* A first indication of the build direction

The player should understand:

> **What kind of team are we becoming?**

---

# Mid-Run Progression

The middle of the run should develop the build.

The players should:

* Combine synergies
* Increase power
* Manage intoxication
* Make riskier decisions

This is where the build should become recognizable.

---

# Late-Run Progression

The late game should test the build.

The team should become:

* Very powerful
* Highly specialized
* Difficult to control

The question becomes:

> **Can this build survive the consequences of its own power?**

---

# Run Failure

Failure should feel understandable.

The team should be able to identify why they lost:

* Too much intoxication
* Poor positioning
* Bad upgrade choices
* Overcommitting to a dangerous fight
* Failure to communicate
* Poor build synergy

Avoid making failure feel completely random.

---

# Meta-Progression

Permanent progression should be added only after the run itself is fun.

Possible meta-progression:

## Unlock New Weapons

New Shooter starting weapons.

---

## Unlock New Carts

Different Rider starting vehicles.

---

## Unlock New Beer Types

Different risk/reward profiles.

---

## Unlock New Upgrade Pools

New build possibilities.

---

## Unlock New Maps

New environments and challenges.

---

# Meta-Progression Philosophy

Permanent progression should expand:

> **Possibility**

rather than simply providing:

> **Power**

Good:

> Unlock a new cart with a different handling model.

Good:

> Unlock a weapon that creates a new build.

Less interesting:

> Permanently gain +5% damage.

The game should not require players to grind permanent upgrades just to become strong enough to play.

The team could also choose a shared modifier.

Examples:

## Cheap Beer

Beer is more common, but intoxication increases faster.

---

## Heavy Cart

More collision damage, but lower acceleration.

---

## Party Mode

Both players gain power from intoxication.

---

# Progression Design Rules

## Rule 1 — Every Upgrade Should Create a Decision

Prefer:

> **Higher speed but worse braking**

over:

> **+10% speed**

---

## Rule 2 — Players Should Discuss Important Choices

The best upgrade choices should make the players ask:

> **What does the team need right now?**

---

## Rule 3 — Both Players Should Have Agency

The Shooter should not receive all meaningful upgrades.

The Rider should not be limited to passive handling bonuses.

---

## Rule 4 — Beer Should Always Matter

Beer should affect:

* Power
* Control
* Builds
* Risk
* Communication

---

## Rule 5 — Stronger Should Also Mean More Difficult

The team's power should create new problems.

This is the game's central progression philosophy.

---

# The Progression Formula

The ideal progression looks like:

```text id="0t8l0y"
Upgrade
    ↓
New Capability
    ↓
New Strategy
    ↓
New Risk
    ↓
New Decision
```

For example:

```text id="8p5b9v"
Turbo Engine
    ↓
Higher Speed
    ↓
Faster Ramming
    ↓
Harder Braking
    ↓
"Do we risk going faster?"
```

---

# Final Progression Goal

At the beginning of a run:

> **The team is trying to survive.**

In the middle of a run:

> **The team is trying to build a strategy.**

At the end of a run:

> **The team is trying to survive the consequences of the strategy they created.**

This is the ideal progression arc.

---

# Core Progression Principle

The game should not simply make the players stronger.

It should make them:

```text id="l6t1hj"
Stronger
    +
More Specialized
    +
More Powerful
    +
More Difficult to Control
```

The best possible outcome is that the players eventually create a build that makes them say:

> **"This is incredibly powerful."**

Immediately followed by:

> **"We have made a terrible mistake."**

That is the progression fantasy of the game.
