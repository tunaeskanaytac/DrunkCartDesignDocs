# Overview
//Drunk Cart// is a chaotic cooperative roguelike centered around an unconventional two-player vehicle: a shopping cart. One player takes the role of the Rider, controlling the cart's movement, momentum, and physical interactions with the environment, while the other becomes the Shooter, sitting in the cart and fighting hordes of enemies. The two players must work together to navigate dangerous environments, position the cart effectively, avoid hazards, ram enemies, and use the Rider's movement to create opportunities for the Shooter. The Rider and Shooter have deliberately different responsibilities, but neither player can fully succeed alone.

Throughout each run, enemies drop beer that the players can collect and drink to become more powerful. Intoxication improves certain abilities—particularly combat power and momentum—but makes the shopping cart increasingly difficult to control, introducing more instability and chaos to the run. This creates a constant risk-versus-reward decision: do the players remain in control, or drink more beer and embrace the chaos for greater power? As the run progresses, the players defeat enemies, choose upgrades, develop powerful builds, and navigate increasingly dangerous environments. The goal is to combine the chaotic physicality of a shopping cart with the replayability of a roguelike, creating a game where cooperation, communication, movement, and the willingness to make increasingly terrible decisions are all equally important.

# Gameplay
The game is a cooperative action roguelike built around two players sharing control of a single shopping cart. Each player has a distinct role: the Rider controls the cart's movement and interacts with the physical environment, while the Shooter attacks enemies and supports the Rider through combat abilities. The players must coordinate their actions to survive increasingly dangerous encounters, using movement, positioning, combat, and environmental interactions to overcome enemies.

## First Minute of Gameplay 
The game begins by immediately placing two players in control of a single shopping cart. One player takes the Rider's seat, learning to accelerate, steer, and drift through a compact arena, while the Shooter experiments with aiming and firing at the first wave of enemies. Within seconds, the players discover that success depends on cooperation—the Rider must position the cart to create clear shots, while the Shooter protects the Rider by eliminating approaching threats.

As the first enemies fall, they drop beer, introducing the game's central risk-versus-reward mechanic. The players are encouraged to take their first drink, experiencing an immediate increase in power alongside the first signs of intoxication as the cart becomes noticeably more difficult to control. The opening minute concludes with the players surviving their first encounter, collecting a reward, and making their first upgrade choice, leaving them eager to discover how much stronger—and how much more chaotic—their run can become.
## Core Gameplay
The Rider controls the shopping cart's acceleration, steering, braking, and momentum. Driving is an active part of the gameplay rather than simply a method of transportation. The Rider is responsible for positioning the cart, avoiding hazards, navigating the environment, maintaining momentum, and using the cart itself as a weapon by ramming enemies and interacting with the world.

The Shooter operates from the cart and independently aims and attacks enemies. The Shooter is responsible for dealing damage, prioritizing threats, and supporting the Rider through combat abilities. Because the Shooter's effectiveness depends partly on the cart's position and movement, the two players must constantly coordinate. The Rider creates opportunities through movement, while the Shooter takes advantage of those opportunities through combat.

The game is designed around the interaction between these two roles. Neither player should feel like they are simply playing a conventional version of their role independently. The Rider and Shooter share the same vehicle, meaning that one player's actions directly affect the other. **A sharp turn can disrupt the Shooter's aim**, while poor positioning can leave the cart exposed to enemies. Conversely, accurate shooting can remove threats that prevent the Rider from moving freely, allowing the team to maintain momentum and reach better positions.

The ideal gameplay experience is not perfect execution. It is two players making increasingly ambitious decisions, attempting to maintain control as their characters become more powerful, and occasionally discovering that they have created a situation that neither of them can handle.

## Combat and Movement
Combat takes place while the cart is constantly moving through the environment. Players must balance offensive actions with survival and positioning. The Rider can use speed and momentum to avoid enemies, navigate around hazards, and ram targets. The Shooter attacks enemies directly and must choose which threats to prioritize.

The environment is an active part of combat. Open areas provide room for maneuvering and maintaining speed, while narrow routes and obstacles make movement more difficult but may offer strategic advantages. Ramps, hazards, destructible objects, and other environmental features create additional opportunities for the Rider to use the cart's physicality to the team's advantage.

The goal is for movement and combat to remain interconnected. The Rider is not simply moving the Shooter between encounters, and the Shooter is not simply fighting enemies while the Rider drives. The effectiveness of each player depends on how well the two roles work together.

## Beer and Intoxication
Enemies can drop beer that the players collect during a run. Beer can be consumed to increase the team's power, but doing so also increases intoxication.

Intoxication creates a risk-and-reward decision. As intoxication increases, the players gain stronger abilities, but the cart becomes increasingly difficult to control. The Rider may gain increased momentum or other advantages while losing precision and stability. The Shooter may become more powerful while facing other disadvantages.

>This creates a central decision during each run:
>
> **How much power are we willing to trade for control?**

The team must decide whether to remain relatively stable and predictable or embrace increasing chaos in exchange for greater power. The effects of intoxication should interact with the environment and the cart's movement, making the consequences of drinking especially noticeable during difficult navigation and combat situations.
## Run Structure
Each run consists of a series of combat encounters, environmental challenges, rewards, and progression choices. The players begin with a relatively simple set of abilities and become stronger throughout the run by defeating enemies, collecting rewards, and selecting upgrades.

As the run progresses, the players develop a build based on their choices. Upgrades can improve the Rider, the Shooter, the cart, their shared abilities, or the effects of intoxication. The goal is to create powerful combinations that allow the players to handle increasingly difficult encounters.

## Progression
The game uses roguelike progression to make each run different. Players gain power during a run by defeating enemies and selecting upgrades. These upgrades can create different builds and alter how the Rider, Shooter, cart, and beer mechanics function.

Progression should encourage experimentation and synergy rather than simply increasing numbers. A player may choose to build around high-speed ramming, powerful ranged attacks, improved cart handling, extreme intoxication, or combinations of these systems.

The central progression tension is that becoming stronger can also make the game more difficult to control. A powerful build may create new problems that the players must learn to manage.

## Victory Conditions
A standard run is considered complete when players defeat the Final Boss, marking the successful completion of the game's intended progression. The run, however, does not end automatically. After defeating the Final Boss, players may choose to end the run or continue into an Endless Mode using their existing build. Endless Mode is designed as an optional post-victory challenge where difficulty continues to escalate beyond the balanced progression of a normal run, allowing players to push increasingly powerful and chaotic builds to their limits. This structure provides every run with a clear objective and satisfying conclusion while still rewarding players who wish to experiment, chase high scores, or simply see how long they can survive after achieving victory.

	Possible upgrades:
	- More acceleration
	- More impact force
	- Larger collision area
	- Knockback
	- Damage scales with speed

	- Ramming generates beer
	- Ramming causes explosions

	A controlled sideways slide that lets the Rider rapidly change direction.

	A short burst of extreme acceleration.
	Unlike Ram, Boost is primarily about movement, although upgrades could make it offensive.
